The cash conversion ratio (operating cash flow / net income) is **1.52x** in 2024 (333/219) — apparently healthy. However, this figure is significantly boosted by the CHF 414 million D&A addback, which represents genuine asset consumption. **Free cash flow** (after capex) is the more relevant metric.

### Key Cash Flow Observations

**The inventory drag is the dominant working capital issue.** The change in inventories consumed CHF 220 million of cash in 2024 — the group produced more than it sold. In 2023, this was an even more severe CHF 698 million drain. The year-on-year improvement (CHF 478 million less drain) reflects reduced production relative to 2023's build-up, but the group is still net investing in inventory despite falling revenues.

**Tax payments (CHF 202 million) exceeded income taxes accrued (CHF 126 million).** This CHF 76 million difference represents prepayment of prior-period tax estimates — common in multi-jurisdiction groups — and will eventually normalize.

## 2. Free Cash Flow

| (CHF million) | 2024 | 2023 |
|---------------|-----:|-----:|
| Cash flow from operating activities | 333 | 615 |
| Less: Capex — PPE | -503 | -730 |
| Less: Capex — Intangibles | -46 | -55 |
| **Free cash flow** | **-216** | **-170** |

Swatch Group generated **negative free cash flow of CHF -216 million** in 2024. This is a critical finding: the operating business, at 2024's volume levels, does not generate enough cash to fund its own capital expenditure programme.

The negative FCF reflects:
1. **Capex maintained at CHF 549 million** (PPE + intangibles) — deliberately elevated to maintain long-term production infrastructure and the retail network, even in a downturn. Capex was reduced from CHF 785 million in 2023 but remains above the depreciation charge of CHF 416 million (net capex = CHF 133 million).
2. **Inventory consuming CHF 220 million** of operating cash that would otherwise be available for capex coverage.

### Capex Breakdown — Maintenance vs. Growth

The group does not separately disclose maintenance versus growth capex. Analysis of the movement schedule (Note 8) suggests:
- **Technical equipment & machinery additions:** CHF 261 million — primarily production equipment maintenance/upgrades
- **Land/buildings additions:** CHF 38 million — primarily existing facility maintenance
- **Other equipment additions:** CHF 115 million — likely retail fit-out and IT
- **Construction in progress additions:** CHF 74 million — growth capex for new facilities

Estimated maintenance capex: approximately CHF 300–350 million (to match depreciation on the existing asset base). Estimated growth capex: approximately CHF 150–200 million. This split suggests the group is funding moderate growth investment even in the downturn — consistent with the management philosophy of maintaining long-term capability.

## 3. Capital Allocation in Context — CHF 600M+ Returned While Earnings Collapsed

The capital allocation decisions of 2024 are striking in context:

| Use of Cash | 2024 (CHF m) | 2023 (CHF m) |
|-------------|------------:|------------:|
| Dividends to shareholders | -335 | -311 |
| Dividends to non-controlling interests | -23 | -17 |
| Purchase of treasury shares | **-50** | 0 |
| **Total shareholder returns** | **-408** | **-328** |
| Operating cash flow | 333 | 615 |
| **Shareholder returns as % of operating CF** | **122%** | **53%** |

In 2024, Swatch Group returned **CHF 408 million to shareholders** while generating only CHF 333 million from operations — a payout ratio relative to operating cash flow of 122%. This was funded by drawing down the group's net liquidity position (from CHF 1,988 million to CHF 1,376 million, a decline of CHF 612 million).

**The dividend of CHF 335 million** was paid for FY2023 earnings (CHF 6.50 per bearer share); the proposed FY2024 dividend of CHF 4.50 per bearer share (total CHF 235 million) will be paid in May 2025 — visible in the financing section of 2025's statement.

**The CHF 50 million in treasury share buybacks** was executed during 2024 — Swatch Group acquired 250,000 bearer shares at an average price of CHF 200.92 per share. Given the year-end price of approximately CHF 165, these shares are underwater relative to acquisition cost by approximately 18%. The buybacks, combined with family open-market purchases, are intended to support the share price and signal management conviction.

### Assessment of Capital Allocation

The decision to maintain dividends and initiate buybacks while generating negative FCF is defensible given the group's CHF 12.2 billion equity base and CHF 1.4 billion net liquidity. However, it represents a deliberate choice to prioritize:
- Signaling financial strength (dividend maintenance) over cash preservation
- Hayek family voting consolidation (buybacks reduce public float, increasing family's relative voting weight) over capital conservation
- Long-term Swiss industrial capacity (continued capex and inventory) over short-term cash optimization

This is consistent with the Hayek family's historically demonstrated long-term orientation — the same approach was taken in 2020 (dividends paid from reserves during a loss year). The risk is that if the downturn persists beyond 2025, cash reserves could be strained: at the 2024 burn rate, the CHF 1,376 million net liquidity provides approximately 3–4 years of buffer before financial stress emerges.

## 4. Investing Activities — Capex Discipline Improving

Total investing outflows improved from CHF -768 million (2023) to CHF -470 million (2024) — a CHF 298 million reduction. The primary driver was capex reduction: PPE investments fell from CHF 730 million to CHF 503 million (-31.1%). The group also reduced net investment in current financial assets/securities (from net CHF 4 million inflow in 2023 to net CHF 89 million inflow in 2024 as securities were partially liquidated to fund operations).

Net liquidity declined CHF 612 million in 2024. At the 2024 annual depletion rate of CHF 612 million, the group has approximately 2.3 years of net liquidity runway before reaching zero. However, this calculation assumes no improvement in operating conditions — which is management's base case for recovery in 2025. The SNB's 125 bps rate cuts in 2024 (taking the policy rate from 1.75% to 0.50%) reduce interest income somewhat (CHF 34 million in 2024, down from CHF 41 million in 2023) but also reflect a more accommodative macro environment expected to support economic activity.

The group's interest income of CHF 34 million on approximately CHF 1.4 billion net cash represents a yield of approximately 2.4% — consistent with Swiss franc deposit rates in 2024. As SNB rates normalize, this income stream will continue to moderate.

## 6. Summary — Cash Flow Quality Assessment

| Metric | 2024 | 2023 |
|--------|-----:|-----:|
| Operating cash flow (CHF m) | 333 | 615 |
| Free cash flow (CHF m) | -216 | -170 |
| Shareholder returns (CHF m) | -408 | -328 |

| Cash conversion (OCF/net income) | 1.52x | 0.69x |
| FCF yield on market cap (2024 MC) | -2.5% | -1.3% |

The 2024 cash flow position is fundamentally stressed. The group is spending more on capex than it earns in operations, returning more to shareholders than it generates from operations, and depleting its liquidity reserves at a significant pace. The fortress balance sheet absorbs this stress comfortably in the short term — but it underscores that the group's financial model requires CHF 7,500 million+ of revenue (roughly 10–15% above current levels) to generate sustainable positive free cash flow without drawing on reserves.
