## 1. Asset Quality — The Fortress Balance Sheet

Swatch Group's balance sheet is exceptionally strong by any conventional measure. The equity ratio of **87.3%** (total equity CHF 12,217 million / total assets CHF 13,992 million) is extraordinarily high for an industrial group — reflecting decades of retained earnings accumulation. Total financial debt stands at just **CHF 22 million** (CHF 20 million current, CHF 2 million non-current), producing a net cash position of CHF 1,081 million.

The balance sheet strength insulates the group from any solvency concern — even at 2024's depressed earnings levels, the group generates positive operating cash flow (CHF 333 million) and faces no refinancing risk. In the context of the operating leverage crisis described in Section 03, this matters enormously: Swatch Group can sustain 2–3 years of subdued earnings without capital markets access.

## 2. The Inventory Controversy — Core Balance Sheet Risk

### Inventory Build Against Revenue Decline

| (CHF million) | 31.12.2024 | 31.12.2023 | Change | % Change |
|---------------|----------:|----------:|-------:|---------:|
| Raw materials & auxiliary supplies | 660 | 690 | -30 | -4.3% |
| Goods in progress | 408 | 565 | -157 | -27.8% |
| Semi-finished goods | 2,287 | 2,117 | +170 | +8.0% |
| **Finished goods** | **3,918** | **3,565** | **+353** | **+9.9%** |
| Spare parts for customer service | 368 | 372 | -4 | -1.1% |
| **Total inventories** | **7,641** | **7,309** | **+332** | **+4.5%** |

The headline fact: inventories increased CHF 332 million (+4.5%) while net sales fell CHF 1,153 million (-14.6%). This is not an accident — it is strategy. Management has explicitly stated its intention to maintain Swiss production capacity and employment rather than cut output to match demand. The production division absorbed costs without generating revenue, running at a deliberately maintained loss.

**The composition shift within inventories is diagnostic:**
- Raw materials fell slightly (-4.3%) — sourcing reduced at the front end
- Goods in progress fell sharply (-27.8%) — active production pipeline slowed
- Semi-finished goods rose (+8.0%) — materials worked into components but not completed watches
- **Finished goods rose +9.9% to CHF 3,918 million** — completed watches that did not find buyers

The CHF 353 million increase in finished goods is the most concerning element. These are completed watches held at production cost, valued against net realizable value per the group's accounting policy. PwC identified this as the sole key audit matter and concluded management's approach is "reasonable" — but the underlying assumption is that these watches will sell at or above production cost in future periods. If China demand remains depressed or if mid-market watch prices deteriorate further, the net realizable value test becomes increasingly sensitive.

### Inventory Write-downs Applied

The group wrote down CHF 47 million gross and reversed CHF 8 million in 2024, for a net income statement impact of CHF -39 million (2023: net -CHF 50 million). While the gross write-down decreased year-on-year despite a larger and more challenging inventory position, this reflects management's assessment that the slowdown is cyclical rather than causing permanent value impairment. The current write-down level of CHF 39 million net against a CHF 7,641 million inventory is less than 0.5% — a relatively modest provision on a portfolio of this size and concentration.

**Audit risk assessment:** If 2025 revenues remain at 2024 levels or decline further, the case for maintaining current NRV assumptions weakens. A 5% inventory impairment would reduce equity by approximately CHF 382 million — equivalent to nearly two years of current net income.

## 3. Goodwill — The Swiss GAAP FER Disclosure

Under Swiss GAAP FER, **goodwill is charged directly to Group equity at acquisition** rather than capitalized on the balance sheet. This is a significant accounting policy difference from IFRS (which requires goodwill capitalization and annual impairment testing).

At 31 December 2024:
- Historical goodwill cost charged to equity: CHF 1,481 million (total); CHF 1,416 million remaining balance in equity
- Theoretical net book value if goodwill had been capitalized (Note 33): CHF 70 million (nearly fully amortized)
- Impact on reported equity: equity is understated by CHF 70 million versus IFRS equivalent

**International comparability note:** Under IFRS, Swatch Group's balance sheet would show approximately CHF 70 million more in goodwill and CHF 70 million more in equity. This is immaterial at the current level of goodwill (near full amortization). However, any future acquisitions would create new goodwill that, under IFRS, would remain on the balance sheet subject to annual impairment testing — potentially a more conservative treatment in a deteriorating market environment.

## 4. Operating Lease Commitments — Off-Balance Sheet Leverage

Swatch Group does not apply IFRS 16 (which would capitalize operating leases on-balance sheet). Under Swiss GAAP FER, operating lease commitments are disclosed in Note 28 as off-balance sheet commitments:

| (CHF million) | 31.12.2024 | 31.12.2023 | Change |
|---------------|----------:|----------:|-------:|
| Less than 1 year | 386 | 336 | +50 |
| Between 1 and 5 years | 1,123 | 1,009 | +114 |
| Over 5 years | 613 | 613 | 0 |
| **Total operating leases** | **2,122** | **1,958** | **+164** |
| Annual lease cost recognized | 551 | 557 | -6 |

The CHF 2,122 million in non-cancellable operating lease commitments primarily represents retail store rentals. **Under IFRS 16 treatment**, these would add approximately CHF 2,122 million (undiscounted) or approximately CHF 1,800–1,900 million (discounted at current rates) to total liabilities, and a corresponding right-of-use asset. This would:
- Reduce the reported equity ratio from 87.3% to approximately 76–78%
- Increase the debt-to-equity ratio from 0.002x to approximately 0.15x
- Add depreciation and interest expense to the income statement (replacing rent expense), changing EBITDA but not net income materially

Even on an IFRS 16-equivalent basis, the balance sheet remains very strong. The key governance concern is that the lease commitment grew CHF 164 million year-on-year despite declining revenues — lease commitments are being expanded or renewed even as the DTC channel faces headwinds. The over-5-year tranche of CHF 613 million (unchanged) represents very long-duration commitments that cannot be exited without penalty.

Net working capital (excluding cash and financial assets) increased CHF 312 million despite the revenue decline. ## 8. Equity Structure

Retained earnings declined CHF 142 million (from 15,416 to 15,274) — dividends of CHF 335 million paid to shareholders exceeded net result attributable to shareholders of CHF 193 million. Translation differences improved CHF 146 million (favorable FX translation of non-CHF assets), partially offsetting the retained earnings drain. The goodwill line deteriorated CHF 64 million — reflecting the Belenos Clean Power acquisition (CHF 59 million goodwill charged to equity) and the Rivoli/MAGRABi transaction reclassification.

Book value per bearer share: **CHF 236.37** (2023: CHF 236.39) — virtually unchanged, demonstrating the equity buffer's resilience despite the earnings collapse. At the year-end bearer price of approximately CHF 165, the stock traded at **0.70x book value** — rare for a luxury goods company and the central valuation signal of this audit.
