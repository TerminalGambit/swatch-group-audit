## The Central Narrative

Swatch Group's FY2024 results present a textbook operating leverage crisis. A 14.6% decline in net sales — equivalent to CHF 1,153 million of lost revenue — produced a 74.5% collapse in operating profit: CHF 887 million wiped from operating results on a revenue reduction of CHF 1,153 million. The operating leverage multiplier was 5.1x: every 1% decline in revenue translated into a 5.1% decline in operating profit.

**This is not primarily a cost control failure.** Swatch Group reduced materials purchases by CHF 519 million and trimmed operating expenses by CHF 76 million. Personnel costs declined CHF 44 million despite an average headcount of 33,230. The fundamental problem is structural: fixed costs of approximately CHF 3,945 million — representing wages, depreciation, maintenance, rents, and energy — constitute 58.6% of net sales. When revenue falls 14.6%, these costs do not fall proportionately, and the entire decline falls through to operating profit.

Three amplifying factors compounded the leverage effect:

**1. China concentration.** Greater China (mainland, Hong Kong SAR, Macau SAR) represented 33% of net sales in 2023 at CHF 2,630 million. In 2024, this region collapsed by approximately CHF 797 million to CHF 1,833 million (-30.4%). This single regional shift accounted for 69% of the group's entire revenue decline. The Swiss watch industry's exports to mainland China fell -25.8% (FH data) — described by the Federation de l'Horlogerie as worse than the COVID-19 demand shock. Swatch Group's exposure was amplified by its high concentration and its mid-market price positioning in a segment that contracted more severely (-15.6% below CHF 3,000 export price vs. +1.0% above CHF 3,000).

**2. Mid-market price positioning.** Swatch Group's revenue base is dominated by brands priced below CHF 3,000 at retail (Tissot, Swatch brand, Longines entry-range, Rado, Hamilton). The Swiss watch industry's bifurcation in 2024 was severe: the below-CHF-3,000 segment contracted -15.6% in value while the above-CHF-3,000 segment grew +1.0%. Swatch holds 72% volume share in the entry/mid segment — this is simultaneously its largest channel and its most vulnerable one.

**3. Olympic year marketing spend.** Omega served as Official Timekeeper at Paris 2024 (31st Olympic appointment, extending through Brisbane 2032). Management explicitly cited Olympic marketing expenditure as a margin headwind. This spend is non-recurring in the Olympic cycle sense, but the IOC contract runs to 2032, creating sustained elevated costs.

The combination of these three factors — a sharp China demand shock falling on a mid-market portfolio with a fixed-cost manufacturing base during a peak marketing spend year — explains the disproportionate margin collapse.

## Three Key Findings

### Finding 1: Operating leverage is extreme and structurally embedded

Swatch Group's strategic commitment to Swiss manufacturing (approximately 150 production facilities, 16,441 employees under Swiss contracts) and its owned retail network (47% of Watches & Jewelry sales through own retail, up from 33% in 2019) creates a cost structure that is highly levered to volume. This strategy is defensible — Swiss Made certification, supply chain control, and DTC margin capture all have strategic merit — but it produces violent earnings swings when revenue declines even modestly. At CHF 7,888 million (2023 revenue), the operating margin was 15.1%. At CHF 6,735 million (2024), it was 4.5%. The breakeven point appears to be approximately CHF 6,200–6,500 million at current cost levels.

### Finding 2: The ETR spike masked a worse underlying earnings quality

The effective tax rate jumped from 22.7% in 2023 to 36.5% in 2024 — a 13.8 percentage point deterioration. The primary driver was non-recognition of CHF 41 million of tax loss carryforwards (Note 27). The group now holds CHF 863 million in unrecognized tax loss carryforwards across its 37 tax jurisdictions, with a potential tax benefit of CHF 152 million if these losses could be utilized. This pool signals that multiple subsidiaries — most likely in Greater China and some European markets — incurred significant local-entity losses in 2024. The ETR distortion reduced net income by approximately CHF 50 million compared to what the applicable rate of 20.9% would have produced.

### Finding 3: Inventory built while revenue fell — a strategic bet with embedded risk

Inventories increased by CHF 332 million (+4.5%) to CHF 7,641 million despite a CHF 1,153 million revenue decline. This is a deliberate management choice: maintaining Swiss manufacturing capacity and employment rather than cutting production to match demand. PwC identified inventory valuation as the sole key audit matter. Inventories now represent 54.6% of total assets. The write-down policy (net -CHF 39 million in 2024) has been applied, but the underlying risk is that finished goods (CHF 3,918 million) and semi-finished goods (CHF 2,287 million) are valued at production cost in a market where net realizable values may be declining for mid-market references. If China demand does not recover in 2025, additional impairment pressure is likely.

## Three Key Risks

### Risk 1: China structural vs. cyclical — the 60/40 diagnosis

This audit's assessment is that approximately 60% of the 2024 deterioration is cyclical (China demand will recover as property wealth destruction stabilizes, consumer confidence recovers, and the anti-ostentation political environment moderates) and approximately 40% is structural (mid-market watch segment faces secular pressure from secondhand market growth, Apple Watch and Chinese smartwatch competition at entry level, and generational preference shifts away from status objects). The structural component means a full revenue recovery to 2023 levels is not guaranteed even if China macro conditions improve.

### Risk 2: Inventory impairment if demand recovery is delayed

At CHF 7,641 million, inventories represent a significant balance sheet concentration. If 2025 revenues remain at 2024 levels or below, the group's Swiss GAF FER inventory valuation policy (lower of cost or NRV) will require accelerated write-downs. The 2024 write-down of CHF 47 million gross suggests management is applying judgment conservatively. A sustained downturn could require CHF 200-400 million in additional write-downs, materially impacting net equity.

### Risk 3: Capital allocation during earnings trough creates governance tension

Swatch Group distributed CHF 335 million in dividends to shareholders plus CHF 50 million in share buybacks in 2024, against operating cash flow of only CHF 333 million. The group is effectively returning more cash than it generates from operations, funded by drawing down its net liquidity from CHF 1,988 million to CHF 1,376 million. Simultaneously, an activist investor sought a board seat in 2025, challenging the Hayek family's 44.1% voting control. The family's dual-class governance structure (44.1% votes on approximately 25% economic capital) insulates strategic decision-making but may prevent necessary capital reallocation in a prolonged downturn.

---

## Auditor's Overall Assessment

The balance sheet remains a fortress: 87.3% equity ratio, CHF 1,376 million net liquidity, and minimal financial debt of CHF 22 million. Solvency risk is essentially zero at current leverage levels. The crisis is one of profitability and cash generation — not financial stability.

The PwC audit opinion is clean and unqualified. The sole key audit matter (inventory valuation) was addressed through appropriate procedures with a conclusion that management's approach is reasonable.

The key question for investors and stakeholders is not whether Swatch Group survives 2024 — it clearly does, with strong liquidity — but whether the current profitability level represents a trough or a new structural floor. This audit finds that a return to 10%+ operating margins requires either (a) China revenue recovery to approximately CHF 2,300 million (from CHF 1,833 million), or (b) meaningful cost restructuring, or (c) successful premium brand repositioning. None of these is certain within a 12-month horizon.
