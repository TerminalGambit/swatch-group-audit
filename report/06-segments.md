## 1. Watches & Jewelry Segment — Deep Dive

### Revenue and Margin Collapse

The Watches & Jewelry segment — comprising all 16 consumer brands and the group's production subsidiaries — saw its operating margin collapse from **17.2% to 6.4%**, a 10.8 percentage point deterioration. In absolute terms, operating result fell from CHF 1,298 million to CHF 410 million, a decline of CHF 888 million (-68.4%).

Revenue fell 14.9% to CHF 6,418 million. Third-party sales declined to CHF 6,415 million (2023: CHF 7,544 million).

### The Production Division — Deliberate Loss Strategy

Within the Watches & Jewelry segment, the group's production division — which manufactures components for both internal brands and third-party Swiss watchmakers — is the hidden lever of the margin collapse. The group has not separately quantified the production segment's results in public reporting, but management confirmed in communications that the production division reported a "strongly negative operating result" in 2024.

The strategic logic: Swatch Group's production subsidiaries (particularly ETA and Nivarox-FAR) maintain the Swiss watchmaking ecosystem by providing movements and components to hundreds of independent brands. When watch demand falls industrywide, these production companies face under-utilization of their fixed manufacturing infrastructure. Rather than cutting production and causing supply disruptions to the ecosystem — which would damage long-term relationships and Swatch Group's reputation as a responsible supplier — management has chosen to absorb the fixed costs and maintain capacity.

The financial cost of this decision is embedded in the W&J margin. If the production division lost approximately CHF 200–400 million in operating terms (consistent with the scale of the capacity maintenance required), the underlying brand-level W&J margin would be in the range of **10–15%** — far less alarming than the reported 6.4%.

**Breguet unit collapse: 20,000 → 7,400 units** (-63%). Breguet sits at the intersection of two problems: it is primarily an ultra-luxury product heavily dependent on Chinese HNWI buyers, and its price point (CHF 10,000–300,000+) makes it especially vulnerable to "luxury shame" sentiment and reduced conspicuous consumption. The revenue decline from approximately CHF 400 million to CHF 165 million (estimated) represents the most severe individual brand deterioration in the group's portfolio.

**Omega** — the commercial anchor at approximately CHF 2,390 million estimated revenue (35% of group sales) — remained relatively resilient compared to Breguet but still declined. Omega's Paris 2024 Olympic Timekeeping role generated significant brand visibility but, per Fortune analysis, did not translate into demand recovery (used Omega prices declined ~1% in the 30 days post-Games while Rolex and Patek rose).

**MoonSwatch and Scuba Fifty Fathoms** were cited by management as relative outperformers. The Swatch brand's collaborations — the MoonSwatch (CHF 250 bioceramic Speedmaster) and the Scuba Fifty Fathoms (CHF 500 range) — continued to generate cultural relevance and volume, demonstrating the strategic value of cross-segment internal collaboration at accessible price points.

### Own Retail — 47% Share and Growing

The group's shift toward direct-to-consumer (DTC) retail accelerated through 2024:
- **2024:** 47% of W&J sales through own retail
- **2019:** 33% of W&J sales through own retail

This 14 percentage point shift over five years represents a fundamental channel transformation. At CHF 6,418 million in W&J sales, 47% own retail equals approximately **CHF 3,016 million sold through Swatch Group's own boutiques and e-commerce**. The strategic benefits (margin capture, brand control, customer data) are real. But in a downturn, own retail becomes a fixed-cost liability: retail leases of CHF 2,122 million must be paid regardless of sales traffic.

The CHF 551 million annual lease cost (Note 28) at 47% DTC share suggests an implied rent-to-sales ratio of approximately 8.2% on the DTC channel — manageable but exposed to further revenue declines.

## 2. Electronic Systems Segment — Order Book Signal

### Revenue and Margin

Electronic Systems (EM Microelectronics and Renata AG) reported:
- Net sales: CHF 330 million (2023: CHF 359 million) — **down 8.1%**
- Operating result: CHF 12 million (2023: CHF 27 million) — **down 55.6%**
- Operating margin: 3.6% (2023: 7.5%) — **down 3.9 pp**

### The Recovery Signal

The headline performance masks a critical forward indicator: **the Electronic Systems order book grew approximately 25% year-on-year** as of year-end 2024. EM Microelectronics produces specialized semiconductors for IoT, wearable devices, and medical applications (not merely watch-related). The demand collapse in 2023–2024 reflected a global semiconductor inventory correction following pandemic-era over-ordering — a pattern now resolving across the industry.

The 25% order book growth suggests that 2025 Electronic Systems revenue could recover meaningfully toward CHF 400–450 million, with operating margin recovering toward the segment's historical 10–15% range. At CHF 330 million current revenue, a 10% margin equates to CHF 33 million operating result — roughly matching the 2022 level. This segment, while small (4.9% of group sales), provides a credible near-term recovery story independent of watch market dynamics.

## 3. Corporate Segment

The Corporate segment includes the holding company costs of Swatch Group AG (parent company). Operating result improved from CHF -134 million to CHF -118 million, a CHF 16 million improvement year-on-year. This improvement likely reflects reduced management bonus accruals (total KMP compensation fell from CHF 34.1 million to CHF 26.9 million) and general overhead controls.

**Greater China = 69% of the group's total revenue decline.** The CHF 797 million reduction in Greater China revenue was the dominant factor — driven by mainland China luxury demand collapse (-25.8% in Swiss watch exports per FH), Hong Kong retail contraction (-18.7% Swiss watch exports), and Macau tourism redistribution away from hard luxury.

China's share of group sales fell from 33% to 27% — a 6 percentage point structural reduction, though management views this as a temporary cyclical shift rather than a permanent rebalancing.

### Bright Spots — USA, Japan, India Records

Management confirmed **record sales** in three markets in 2024:

| Market | Performance | Context |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **USA** | Record sales | USD appreciation (+7.8% vs CHF) made Swiss goods cheaper; Swiss watch exports to US +5.0% (FH) |
| **Japan** | Record sales (+30%) | JPY weakness made Japan's luxury prices 20–30% cheaper than China; 36.87M tourist arrivals (record) |
| **India** | Record sales | Swiss watch exports to India +25.2%; emerging luxury middle class |

Tissot specifically exceeded USD 100 million in US sales for the first time. The USA, Japan, and India records demonstrate that Swatch Group's mid-market brands have genuine demand strength in markets without China's structural headwinds.

### Americas — Resilience

Total Americas sales declined only CHF 10 million (-0.8%) to CHF 1,188 million. This resilience reflects:
- Strong US performance (record sales), partially offset by modest declines in Latin America
- USD/CHF appreciation providing a favorable translation effect
- Tissot's mid-market positioning resonating with the aspirational American consumer

## 5. Structural vs. Cyclical Assessment

The central question for investors is whether the 2024 geographic and segment performance reflects:

**Cyclical factors (60% of the decline, this audit's assessment):**
- China property wealth destruction — structural recovery expected within 2–3 years as government stimulus takes effect
- Swiss franc strength amplifying revenue in CHF terms (-2.4% FX headwind on reported sales)
- Olympic year marketing spend (non-recurring at this intensity level)
- Post-pandemic consumer spending patterns normalizing

**Structural factors (40% of the decline):**
- Swatch Group's 18.3% market share of the Swiss watch industry declined 200 basis points in 2024, while the Big Four private independents (Rolex, Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet, Richard Mille) gained 300 basis points to 47% combined — suggesting genuine brand repositioning pressure
- The below-CHF-3,000 price segment (where Swatch Group dominates by volume) contracted -15.6% versus +1.0% for above CHF 3,000 — a structural bifurcation that disadvantages Swatch's portfolio
- Breguet's unit collapse (20,000 → 7,400) has structural elements: brand equity in ultra-luxury is both the hardest to build and the most fragile when sentiment shifts
- Pre-owned watch market growth (+10% globally in 2024, projected to equal primary market within 10 years) structurally competes with new Omega, Longines, and Tissot sales

**Conclusion:** A China cyclical recovery will restore approximately 60% of the lost revenue and operating result. The remaining 40% requires either structural portfolio repositioning (elevating brand equity at Omega, Harry Winston, Breguet toward true ultra-luxury positioning) or geographic diversification (India, Southeast Asia, Americas) to compensate for China's potential structural plateau.
