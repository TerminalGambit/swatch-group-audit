### DuPont Insight

The ROE decline from 7.3% to 1.8% decomposed as follows:
- **Net margin contribution:** collapsed from 11.28% to 3.25% — the primary driver (-8.03 pp on margin)
- **Asset turnover contribution:** fell from 0.559x to 0.477x (-0.082x) — sales volume decline outpaced asset reduction, reflecting the fixed asset base
- **Equity multiplier:** barely changed (1.145 vs 1.161) — the equity ratio remained essentially flat, confirming this is not a leverage change story

The DuPont analysis confirms that the ROE collapse is **entirely a profitability event** — not a leverage or efficiency story. The balance sheet structure is almost unchanged. Asset turnover declined modestly. The problem is exclusively that margins collapsed.

**Structural observation:** Swatch Group runs with an equity multiplier of only 1.145x — essentially no financial leverage. A typical industrial company with 2.0–2.5x equity multiplier would generate ROE of 3.5–4.5% on the same underlying profitability. The group's conservatism with debt is a double-edged sword: it provides exceptional downside protection but depresses ROE in all but the best years.

## DIO Red Flag — Days Inventory Outstanding

The Days Inventory Outstanding is the most diagnostically alarming ratio in this analysis. Using the group's material purchases as a COGS proxy:

| | 2024 | 2023 |
|-|-----:|-----:|
| Materials consumed (purchases minus inventory change) | CHF 1,132m | CHF 1,177m |
| Average inventory | CHF 7,475m | CHF 6,954m |
| Inventory turnover | 0.151x | 0.169x |
| **Days Inventory Outstanding** | **~2,413 days** | **~2,160 days** |

At face value, these numbers — implying the group holds nearly 6.5 years of inventory — appear extreme. This requires careful interpretation:

1. **The materials-only COGS basis understates actual cost.** Swatch Group's income statement format separates personnel, depreciation, and overhead from materials. If all production costs were included (estimated total cost of goods manufactured approximately CHF 4,000–4,500 million), DIO would fall to approximately 600–700 days — still very high but reflecting the deliberate strategic inventory position.

2. **The inventory includes semi-finished goods and raw materials in the production pipeline** — not just finished goods. Finished goods DIO (CHF 3,918m / daily materials consumption of CHF 3.1m) = ~1,264 days of finished goods at materials cost alone — implying approximately 3.5 years of finished goods.

3. **Management strategy.** This is not inefficiency — it is explicit policy. Swatch Group's integrated manufacturing and component supply role means maintaining significant work-in-progress. Swiss Made watches require 9–18 months from raw material to finished product for complex timepieces.

**Nevertheless:** The DIO metric is worsening significantly (2,160 → 2,413 days) because inventory grew (+4.5%) while the implied sales volume declined (-14.6%). Each additional quarter of demand weakness extends DIO further. If this trend persists, the NRV write-down risk escalates materially.

## Liquidity Assessment — Is the Balance Sheet Safe?

**Short answer: Yes, unambiguously.**

- Current ratio of 9.79x (compared to an industry threshold of 1.5–2.0x for concern) provides an extraordinary liquidity buffer
- Quick ratio of 2.31x means even without selling any inventory, current liabilities are covered more than twice over by liquid assets
- Cash ratio of 1.08x means cash alone covers all current liabilities
- Total financial debt of CHF 22 million against an equity base of CHF 12.2 billion renders financial leverage negligible
- Interest coverage of 101.3x — even at 2024's depressed earnings levels — represents no risk of financial distress

The balance sheet is not stressed; the income statement and cash flows are. The distinction matters for risk assessment: this is a profitability trough, not a financial crisis.
