# 02 — Company Overview
## Swatch Group AG

---

## Corporate Identity

Swatch Group AG is the world's largest watchmaking group by volume and the second largest by revenue. Headquartered in Biel/Bienne, Switzerland, the group was founded in 1983 through the merger of ASUAG and SSIH under the leadership of the late Nicolas G. Hayek Sr. The consolidated group encompasses 148 companies across 37 tax jurisdictions, employing 32,477 people as of 31 December 2024 (33,230 average). Financial reporting follows Swiss GAAP FER with statutory audit by PricewaterhouseCoopers AG, Basel.

The group's business combines the creation, production, and sale of watches and jewelry with the manufacture of electronic components (Electronic Systems segment). It operates across 16 active watch and jewelry brands plus component manufacturing subsidiaries supplying the broader Swiss watch industry.

## Vertical Integration — A Core Strategic Differentiator

Swatch Group's most distinctive competitive advantage is the depth of its vertical integration. Approximately 150 production facilities in Switzerland manufacture not only finished watches but virtually every component — from balance springs and hairsprings to electronic modules, dials, hands, cases, straps, and jewels. Key subsidiaries include:

- **ETA SA** — movement manufacturer supplying movements to Swatch Group brands and, until supply restrictions, to third-party Swiss watchmakers
- **Nivarox-FAR** — hairspring and escapement manufacturer (a near-monopoly position in Swiss Made hairsprings)
- **Asulab** — research laboratory
- **EM Microelectronics** — semiconductors and microcontrollers (part of Electronic Systems segment)
- **Renata AG** — batteries for watches and medical devices

This integration creates multiple structural advantages: supply chain control and certainty of "Swiss Made" certification, cost efficiency through internalization of supplier margins, and technology leadership in precision manufacturing. It also creates the operating leverage problem documented in this audit — the fixed costs of Swiss manufacturing cannot be readily scaled down when demand falls.

The group's 2024 capital expenditure of CHF 503 million on PPE (reduced from CHF 730 million in 2023) reflects continued investment in this infrastructure. Net PPE stood at CHF 3,162 million at 31 December 2024, with gross historical cost of CHF 8,533 million against accumulated depreciation of CHF 5,371 million.

## Governance — Hayek Family Control

Swatch Group operates under a dual-class share structure that concentrates control in the founding family:

| Share Class | Nominal Value | Shares Outstanding | Price Ratio | Votes |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|-------|
| Registered (UHRN) | CHF 0.45 | 116,919,500 | ~1/5 of bearer | 1 vote each |
| Bearer (UHR) | CHF 2.25 | 28,936,000 | 5× registered | 1 vote each |

The **Hayek Pool** — comprising the community of heirs of Marianne and Nicolas G. Hayek (represented by Nayla Hayek), Wat Holding AG, Ammann Group Holding AG, and the Swatch Group Pension Fund — held **44.1% of all voting rights** as of 31 December 2024 (up from 43.3% in 2023). Since registered shares carry the same voting rights as bearer shares at one-fifth the economic value, the family converts approximately 25% economic capital into 44% voting control.

**Board leadership:**
- **Nayla Hayek** — Chairwoman (since June 30, 2010); represents family continuity at board level
- **Nick Hayek Jr.** — CEO (since January 1, 2003); son of the founder and architect of the modern group
- **Marc A. Hayek** — joined Board of Directors at the Annual General Meeting in **May 2024**, formalizing third-generation succession planning. Marc Hayek is the CEO of Breguet and Blancpain

This governance structure provides exceptional strategic stability and long-term orientation — characteristics demonstrated by the decision to maintain Swiss manufacturing capacity and employment through the 2024 downturn rather than restructure. The family's July 2024 open-market purchase of 80,371 bearer shares at CHF 178.30 per share (total CHF 14.3 million, involving Nayla, Nick, and Marc Hayek) signaled conviction that the market undervalued the group. However, the dual-class structure limits external accountability: in 2025, an activist investor reportedly sought a board seat to challenge family control.

**Key management compensation** declined appropriately with performance: total KMP compensation fell from CHF 34.1 million in 2023 to CHF 26.9 million in 2024, with bonuses specifically reduced from CHF 16.2 million to CHF 10.6 million (-35%) — aligned with financial outcomes (Note 29).

The Watches & Jewelry segment encompasses all 16 consumer brands and the production units supplying components to other Swiss watch manufacturers. Excluding the embedded production segment loss, the brand-level W&J margin would be higher.

## Retail Strategy — The DTC Shift

A significant strategic transformation of the past decade has been the expansion of Swatch Group's direct-to-consumer (DTC) retail footprint. In 2019, approximately 33% of Watches & Jewelry sales flowed through own retail. By 2024, this had risen to **47%**. This structural shift increases gross margin capture (eliminating wholesale and retail intermediary margins) and improves brand control, but also:

1. Raises the fixed-cost base — retail leases represent CHF 2,122 million in non-cancellable operating lease commitments (Note 28), with annual rent costs of CHF 551 million
2. Increases exposure to consumer traffic — when demand falls, high-street boutiques become a fixed-cost drain
3. Creates lease commitments that extend beyond demand cycles (1–5 year: CHF 1,123 million; over 5 years: CHF 613 million)

## Research & Development

The group invested CHF 273 million in research and development in 2024 (4.1% of net sales, up from 3.5% in 2023 in percentage terms due to the denominator effect). R&D investment was broadly maintained in absolute terms (CHF 275 million in 2023), demonstrating commitment to technology despite the revenue decline. Key areas include silicon escapements, bioceramic materials, electronic timing systems, and connected watch development.

The geographic concentration risk in Greater China — which fell from 33% of net sales in 2023 to 27% in 2024 — is the defining factor of the 2024 financial results and the central risk for 2025 forecasting.
