# 08 — Industry Benchmarking
## Swatch Group AG vs. Peers and Swiss Watch Industry — FY2024

---

## 1. Swiss Watch Export Industry Context

### FH 2024 Annual Statistics — The Macro Backdrop

The Fédération de l'Horlogerie published its full-year 2024 statistics on 30 January 2025. The Swiss watch industry contracted for the first time since 2020:

| Metric | 2024 | 2023 | Change |
|--------|-----:|-----:|-------:|
| Total export value | CHF 26.0 billion | CHF 26.7 billion | **-2.8%** |
| Export volume (units) | 15.3 million | 16.9 million | **-9.4%** |

The value decline (-2.8%) was much milder than the volume decline (-9.4%), confirming that the industry's average export price continued to rise even as unit volumes fell. This reflects the structural premiumization trend — the watch industry is selling fewer watches at higher prices. This bifurcation has different implications for different players.

### Swatch Group vs. Industry: An 11.8 pp Revenue Underperformance

| Entity | 2024 Revenue Change |
|--------|-------------------:|
| Swatch Group | **-14.6%** |
| Swiss watch industry exports | -2.8% |
| **Underperformance gap** | **-11.8 pp** |

Swatch Group's -14.6% revenue decline was 11.8 percentage points worse than the Swiss watch industry's -2.8% export decline. This gap requires explanation.

**Four factors amplify Swatch's underperformance versus the industry:**

1. **China concentration:** Greater China was 27–33% of Swatch Group's sales vs. a lower percentage for the industry overall. China exports fell -25.8% (FH) — this outsized exposure hit Swatch disproportionately.

2. **Mid-market price mix:** The Swiss industry's below-CHF-3,000 segment (Swatch's largest by volume) fell -15.6% while above-CHF-3,000 rose +1.0%. Swatch's revenue is weighted toward mid-market brands (Tissot, Swatch, Longines entry, Hamilton) in the affected segment.

3. **Breguet unit collapse:** Breguet went from ~20,000 units to 7,400 units, a -63% decline significantly worse than the industry average, representing an idiosyncratic brand-specific issue within the ultra-luxury space.

4. **Currency:** Swatch Group reports in CHF. Its actual operations decline at constant FX was -12.2%; the additional -2.4% came from CHF translation effects.

## 2. Peer Margin Comparison

### Operating Margin Benchmarks (2024)

| Company | Segment | Operating Margin 2024 | Revenue Change 2024 |
|---------|---------|----------------------:|--------------------:|
| **Swatch Group (consolidated)** | All | **4.5%** | -14.6% |
| Swatch Group (Watches & Jewelry segment) | Watches only | 6.4% | -14.9% |
| **Richemont Specialist Watchmakers** | Watches only (FY2025) | **5.3%** | -13.0% |
| Richemont Group total | Group | 20.9% | +4.0% |
| **LVMH Watches & Jewelry** | Watches + Jewelry | **14.7%** | -2% (organic) |
| Kering "Other Houses" | Mixed | Negative | -7.0% |
| Hermès | Group | 40.5% | +15.0% |

**The most important comparison:** Richemont's Specialist Watchmakers segment (IWC, Panerai, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Vacheron Constantin, A. Lange & Söhne) posted a 5.3% operating margin in FY2025 on a -13% revenue decline. This is nearly identical to Swatch Group's watch-segment margin collapse trajectory (17.2% → 6.4%). **The data shows that pure-play premium watchmakers faced industry-wide margin compression — not Swatch-specific mismanagement.**

The contrast with LVMH (14.7%) and Richemont group level (20.9%) illustrates the jewellery advantage: both groups have major high-margin jewelry businesses (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Tiffany, Bulgari) that insulate group margins from watch weakness. Swatch Group — despite Harry Winston — is overwhelmingly a watch company.

**Hermès at 40.5%** remains the luxury industry benchmark for what exceptional brand positioning and supply constraint management can achieve. It is not a directly comparable business but sets the aspirational standard.

## 3. Price Segment Analysis — The Structural Bifurcation

The FH 2024 data reveals a structural divide that is the defining feature of the current market environment:

| Price Segment (export price) | 2024 Performance | Notes |
|------------------------------|:----------------:|-------|
| Below CHF 3,000 | **-15.6%** | Volume and value both fell sharply |
| Above CHF 3,000 | **+1.0%** | Value held/slightly grew |
| Steel watches | -9.8% | — |
| Precious metal watches | +2.2% | — |
| Ultra-luxury (>CHF 50,000) | **+84% of total export growth** | Accounted for 33.5% of total value |

**Implication for Swatch Group:** The group's revenue is primarily in the below-CHF-3,000 export price bracket (Tissot, Swatch brand, Longines entry, Hamilton, Certina, Mido). This segment contracted -15.6% — consistent with the group's reported -14.6% total revenue decline. The above-CHF-3,000 segment (Omega, Longines high range, Rado premium) partially offset this through relative resilience. Harry Winston and Breguet theoretically participate in the ultra-luxury growth but faced China-specific demand collapse that outweighed the global trend.

The bifurcation thesis: consumers are either buying entry-level watches (sub-CHF 500) or ultra-luxury watches (above CHF 10,000). The "aspirational luxury" middle — CHF 1,000–5,000 where Longines, Tissot premium, and Omega entry-level compete — is being squeezed by the secondhand market (providing used Omega and Rolex at 40–60% below retail) and by the rising threshold for signaling social status in a polarizing luxury market.

## 4. Swiss Watch Industry Market Share Analysis

### Group Market Share (Morgan Stanley/LuxeConsult Top 50, 2024)

| Group | 2024 Market Share | 2023 Market Share | Change |
|-------|:-----------------:|:-----------------:|:------:|
| Rolex Group (private) | 32% | ~31% | +~100 bps |
| **Swatch Group** | **18.3%** | **~20.3%** | **-200 bps** |
| Big Four private brands (Rolex, Patek, AP, Richard Mille) | 47% | ~44% | +300 bps |

**Swatch Group lost 200 basis points of market share in a single year.** This is the most concerning structural indicator in this audit. Market share loss in a contracting industry compounds — it means Swatch is declining faster than the market, suggesting the group is not just facing cyclical headwinds but also losing competitive ground.

The Big Four's 300 bps gain to 47% combined share reflects the polarization thesis: ultra-luxury watches from Rolex, Patek Philippe, Audemars Piguet, and Richard Mille gained share because their pricing strategies (controlled supply, price increases, waitlists) make their products inflation-resistant status objects rather than consumption goods. These brands do not need China to grow — their client base is sufficiently global and HNW that regional demand shocks are diversified away.

### Brand-Level Market Position (Estimated)

| Brand | Group | Est. Revenue (CHF m) | Units | Est. Market Share |
|-------|-------|--------------------:|------:|:-----------------:|
| Rolex | Private | 10,583 | 1,176,000 | 32% |
| Omega | Swatch Group | ~2,390 | 505,000 | ~7% |
| Patek Philippe | Private | ~2,300 | 72,000 | ~7% |
| Audemars Piguet | Private | ~2,380 | 51,000 | ~7% |
| Breguet | Swatch Group | ~165 | 7,400 | <1% |

**Omega vs. Rolex:** Omega's estimated 505,000 units at an average price of ~CHF 4,700 generates approximately CHF 2,390 million in revenue. Rolex's 1,176,000 units at CHF 13,140 average generates CHF 10,583 million. Rolex generates 4.4x Omega's revenue at 2.3x the volume — purely through its superior average selling price, exclusivity management, and waitlist dynamics. Omega's strategic challenge is clear: it competes at a price point where secondhand Rolex watches (CHF 6,000–15,000 used Submariner) represent aspirational alternatives that a CHF 5,000 new Omega Seamaster struggles to match in perceived value.

## 5. Rolex Benchmark — The Private Standard

Rolex Group (including Tudor) is privately held and does not publish financials. The Morgan Stanley/LuxeConsult estimates provide the most authoritative public benchmark:

| Metric | Rolex (est.) | Swatch Group | Ratio |
|--------|-------------:|-------------:|------:|
| Revenue (CHF m) | 10,583 | 6,735 | Rolex 1.57x Swatch |
| Units | 1,176,000 | n/a (multiple brands) | — |
| Average price (CHF) | 13,140 | n/a (blended est. ~CHF 2,000) | 6.6x higher |
| Market share | 32% | 18.3% | — |

**Rolex's revenue (CHF 10.58 billion) exceeds Swatch Group's by 57%** — from a single brand with deliberate production constraints versus Swatch Group's 16-brand portfolio. This comparison illustrates the economic value of ultra-premium positioning with supply management: Rolex generates more revenue per unit (CHF 13,140 average price) through deliberate scarcity, while Swatch Group's blended average price across all brands is approximately CHF 2,000.

Rolex reportedly grew revenue primarily by raising average prices (approximately +8%) rather than increasing volume — the opposite strategic lever from Swatch Group's volume-driven model.

## 6. Structural vs. Cyclical Assessment — Final Verdict

| Factor | Classification | Evidence |
|--------|:-------------:|---------|
| China demand collapse | 60% cyclical | Swiss industry-wide: -25.8% China exports. Macroeconomic drivers (property, confidence) will recover |
| Mid-market price compression | 40% structural | FH data: below CHF 3,000 -15.6% is a multi-year trend, not 2024-specific |
| Swatch market share loss (-200 bps) | 50% structural | Big Four gaining consistently; ultra-luxury polarization is secular |
| Breguet unit collapse | 70% cyclical | Ultra-HNWI demand extremely China-sensitive; will recover with China |
| Omega stagnation | 50% structural | Secondhand market growth and Rolex competition are structural headwinds |
| Operating leverage crisis | Neutral | This is a financial structure issue — a strategic choice with pros and cons |

**Overall assessment:** This audit concurs with the 60%/40% cyclical/structural split published in benchmarks. The cyclical recovery narrative has empirical support — Richemont's watch segment also fell 13% (near-identical to Swatch) — confirming a shared market headwind rather than company-specific failure. However, the 40% structural component means that even a full China recovery will not restore Swatch Group to peak 2023 margins without additional strategic action. The mid-market squeeze, secondhand competition, and ultra-luxury bifurcation will persist regardless of China's trajectory.
