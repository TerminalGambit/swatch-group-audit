## 1. Gross Margin Analysis

Swatch Group's income statement format under Swiss GAAP FER does not present a traditional "Cost of Goods Sold" line. Instead, revenues are netted against material purchases and adjusted for inventory movements, producing a production-format P&L. The effective production gross margin — net sales plus inventory changes minus material purchases — declined modestly:

- **2024:** 6,735 + 213 – 1,345 = CHF 5,603m (83.2% of net sales)
- **2023:** 7,888 + 687 – 1,864 = CHF 6,711m (85.1% of net sales)

The apparent gross margin compression of 1.9 percentage points is driven by two factors:
1. **Inventory build reduced margin enhancement:** In 2023, the +CHF 687 million inventory build added 8.7% to reported margin. In 2024, the +CHF 213 million build added only 3.2% — a 5.5 pp swing. The inventory addition represents production that did not result in sales (capacity maintained for future demand).
2. **Materials efficiency partially offset:** Material purchases fell CHF 519 million (-27.8%), more than proportionate to the 14.6% revenue decline, suggesting effective procurement discipline and some mix shift.

The key insight is that Swatch Group's vertically integrated structure means that "gross margin" in the conventional sense is not the right lens — the group produces nearly all its own components, so the material purchase line understates true production cost significantly (personnel, depreciation, and utilities for production are captured in separate lines).

The "Other operating expenses" line spiked from CHF 75 million to CHF 213 million — a CHF 138 million increase that requires explanation. This category likely absorbed significant one-off items including the full Olympic Games Paris marketing deployment costs (rights fees, on-site operations, athlete sponsorships, media campaigns for Omega) that were not captured in the marketing/sales line. Management cited "Olympic marketing spend" as one of three explicit margin headwinds for 2024.

### Fixed Cost Rigidity

Three cost lines are substantially fixed regardless of volume:
- **Personnel:** CHF 2,506 million — fell only CHF 44 million (-1.7%) on a 14.6% revenue decline. Swiss labor law and the Hayek family's commitment to Swiss employment prevent rapid headcount reduction. Personnel costs are 37.2% of net sales in 2024 vs. 32.3% in 2023 — a 4.9 pp deterioration caused by the denominator effect.
- **Depreciation & amortization:** CHF 416 million — increased CHF 26 million (+6.7%) as the prior capex cycle (CHF 803 million in 2023) feeds through to depreciation. Non-cash but represents the economic cost of the fixed asset base.
- **Maintenance, rents & energy:** CHF 1,023 million — declined only CHF 34 million (-3.2%) despite a 14.6% revenue fall. Retail lease commitments (CHF 2,122 million outstanding) are contractually non-cancellable.

Total fixed/semi-fixed cost base: approximately CHF 3,945 million. This represents 58.6% of FY2024 net sales — the core driver of operating leverage.

The effective tax rate of 36.5% is 15.6 percentage points above the applicable rate of 20.9% — a CHF 54 million overcharge versus a "normal" tax burden. The dominant driver is non-recognition of tax loss carryforwards (CHF -41 million impact), reflecting that multiple subsidiaries — almost certainly in Greater China and Hong Kong — incurred significant local losses in 2024 that cannot be reliably recognized as deferred tax assets.

**The unrecognized carryforward pool now stands at CHF 863 million**, up from CHF 726 million in 2023. The potential tax asset that remains unrecognized is CHF 152 million (2023: CHF 126 million). If and when profitable conditions return in those jurisdictions, recognition of these deferred tax assets would provide a meaningful earnings tailwind. Conversely, if losses persist, additional losses will expand this pool further.

### Quantified ETR Impact

At the applicable rate of 20.9%, the tax charge on CHF 345 million pre-tax income would be CHF 72 million, yielding net income of CHF 273 million. The actual net income was CHF 219 million. The ETR spike alone reduced net income by approximately CHF 54 million — equivalent to CHF 1.88 per bearer share. This is material against reported EPS of CHF 3.74.

## 4. Waterfall Analysis — CHF 887 Million Operating Result Decline

The operating result declined from CHF 1,191 million to CHF 304 million. The decomposition of this CHF 887 million decline:

| Driver | Impact (CHF m) | Notes |
|--------|---------------:|-------|
| Revenue volume/price decline | -1,153 | CHF 1,153m revenue lost at ~84% contribution margin = ~-969m gross impact |
| Inventory build reduction | -474 | Add-back fell from +687 to +213; represents production maintained without sales |
| Materials cost reduction | +519 | Purchase volumes fell as production was partially trimmed |
| Personnel cost reduction | +44 | Minimal flexibility; CHF 44m saving on 14.6% revenue decline |
| Other operating expense reduction | +76 | Modest discipline; maintenance/rents barely moved |
| Depreciation increase | -22 | PPE depreciation +CHF 22m from elevated 2023 capex |
| Other operating income increase | +127 | Rose from CHF 136m to CHF 263m; includes Rivoli gain CHF 7m, FX items, government grants |
| **Net change in operating result** | **-887** | **2023: 1,191 → 2024: 304** |

The dominant factor is the interaction between revenue decline and inventory movements. Revenue fell CHF 1,153 million, but because the group continued producing (maintaining capacity), the "changes in inventories" line added only CHF 213 million (vs. CHF 687 million in 2023) — a net reduction in P&L contribution of CHF 474 million. This represents the economic cost of the capacity maintenance strategy: CHF 474 million of production absorbed into inventory rather than sold.

### Olympic Games Marketing Impact

While Swatch Group does not separately disclose Olympic marketing expenditure, the circumstantial evidence is significant. The "Other operating expenses" sub-line jumped from CHF 75 million to CHF 213 million (+CHF 138 million). Industry analysts estimate Olympic timekeeping partnerships of Omega's scale involve annual commitments of CHF 100–200 million when rights fees, on-site operations, and marketing campaigns are combined. The Paris 2024 Games represented an Olympic year — the peak in a four-year cycle of Olympic-related costs. This spend is embedded in the recurring cost structure (the Omega–IOC partnership extends to Brisbane 2032) but is heightened in Olympic years.

The primary driver of the swing was the **net currency result improving from -CHF 91 million to -CHF 5 million** (+CHF 86 million). In 2023, the CHF's strength against Asian currencies (CNY, JPY, HKD) created large translation losses on cross-currency monetary positions. In 2024, currency volatility was lower in this regard. This windfall partially offset the operating result decline.

## 6. Summary — Earnings Quality Assessment

The 2024 net income of CHF 219 million is structurally weaker than the reported number suggests:
- **ETR distortion:** -CHF 54 million versus normalized tax burden
- **Currency tailwind:** +CHF 80 million net financial improvement (non-operating, non-recurring)
- **Non-cash inventory add-back:** CHF 213 million of P&L benefit from capacity maintenance (builds balance sheet risk)
- **One-off Rivoli gain:** CHF 7 million in Other operating income (non-recurring)

Adjusting for these factors, underlying normalized operating earnings approximate CHF 220–270 million — broadly consistent with reported net income but through different mechanisms. The operating profit of CHF 304 million is the cleaner metric: a genuine, cash-backed measure of business performance before financing and tax distortions.
