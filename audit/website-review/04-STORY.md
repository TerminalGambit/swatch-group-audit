# Story Walk-Through Audit
**Reviewer:** story-auditor
**Verdict:** Has structure — the dashboard tells a complete story by itself, but the multi-page journey is more "anthology" than "novel"
**Overall story score:** 6.5 / 10

## Executive summary
The Dashboard is genuinely cinematic — "The Operating Leverage Crisis" headline, the 5.1× callout, six red flags, the PwC auditor's quote, and a CTA footer collectively deliver a full thesis in one viewport. The remaining five pages each carry their own strong headline ("Three Statements, One Crisis", "Two Segments. One Country Did the Damage.", "Numbers that explain the collapse", "Swatch underperformed — but not in isolation", "Read the audit") but they sit as parallel essays rather than chapters that hand off to each other. The journey has a powerful **Act 1** and a powerful **Act 5**, but **Acts 2–4 don't escalate toward a climax** — they just elaborate the verdict the Dashboard already gave away.

## The narrative arc — does it work?

The intended arc — *Setup → Evidence → Cause → Diagnostic → Context → Deep read* — is logical on paper and roughly survives in execution. The Dashboard frames the crisis (the "what"), Financials proves the income statement collapse (the "how big"), Segments localizes it to one country (the "where"), Ratios decomposes ROE and quantifies operating leverage (the "why"), Benchmarking shows peers held up better (the "so what"), and the Report delivers the full nine-section verdict (the "now what"). When a viewer follows the navbar in order, the story does build.

But the experience has three structural weaknesses. **First**, the Dashboard front-loads the punchline — the 5.1× operating leverage callout, the six red flags, and the PwC unqualified-opinion line are all *resolution-grade* material delivered in the first 90 seconds. By the time the viewer reaches Ratios — which is where 5.1× is supposed to be *derived* — the number is already a known quantity, so the page reads as confirmation rather than revelation. **Second**, the page handoffs are weak. The Dashboard has a CTA footer (good), but Financials, Segments, Ratios, and Benchmarking end without "next page" prompts in the visible viewport — the user has to go back to the navbar each time, which breaks narrative flow. **Third**, there is no engineered climax between page 1 and page 6. The Report is a long-form artifact (good for depth), not a dramatic crescendo. The audit-verdict moment never gets its own scene.

The narrative is also slightly **decapitated at the end**: page 6 begins with "Executive Summary" and a wall of prose. There is no "Conclusion" page that re-states the thesis, shows the 5.1× one last time, and tells the reader what to *do* with this — buy/hold/sell, qualified/unqualified, sustainable/structural. The Dashboard's emotional center never gets paid off with an emotional resolution.

## Three persona walk-throughs

### Persona A — Student presenter (5 min)

**Suggested script (click-by-click):**

- **0:00–0:45 — Dashboard (top half):** "Swatch had a 14.6% revenue decline that produced a 74.5% operating profit collapse. Four KPI cards — three red, one green: revenue down, operating result down, net margin at 3.3%, but equity ratio at 87.3%. So this isn't a solvency story — it's an *earnings* story." *(Points at the trend chart.)* "Pre-COVID peak in 2019 never recovered. 2024 is a structural step-down, not a cyclical dip."
- **0:45–1:30 — Dashboard (middle):** *(Points at the 5.1× callout.)* "Here is the thesis in one number. Every 1% of revenue lost became 5.1× of operating profit lost. Why? Fixed costs are 58.6% of sales — personnel, depreciation, rents, energy. When revenue drops, those costs don't move. Breakeven is somewhere between CHF 6.2–6.5 billion. They came in at 6.7."
- **1:30–2:15 — Dashboard (bottom half):** "Six red flags, color-coded by severity. Two high — ETR spike to 36.5% and the CHF 7.6B inventory build during a revenue-down year. Three medium — negative free cash flow, off-balance-sheet leases, market share loss. And one low-medium — the 120% payout ratio. And critically, PwC issued an *unqualified* opinion — the only Key Audit Matter is inventory valuation, which is the same red flag the dashboard already flagged."
- **2:15–3:00 — Click Segments:** "Two segments. One country did the damage." *(Points at headline.)* "Watches & Jewelry is 95% of revenue, operating margin 6.4% — down 10.8 points. Electronic Systems is rounding error at 3.6% margin. Greater China revenue fell 30.4%, from CHF 2.63B to CHF 1.83B — that single country explains roughly 69% of the group's revenue decline. The US set a record, but it's only 18% of mix, so it can't compensate."
- **3:00–3:45 — Click Ratios:** "DuPont decomposition: ROE 1.80%, down from 4.16%. Net margin 3.30%, asset turnover 0.477×, equity multiplier 1.145×. The collapse is *entirely* margin-driven — turnover and leverage barely moved. Operating leverage multiplier here: 5.1×. Interest coverage 101× — they're still rock solid on debt. Liquidity ratios all healthy. The structural problem is profitability, not survival."
- **3:45–4:30 — Click Benchmarking:** "Swatch underperformed but not in isolation. Swiss watch exports down 2.8% overall; Swatch down 14.6% — they lost share to the Big Four — Rolex, Patek, AP, Richemont — by 300 bps. So part of this is industry cycle, part of this is Swatch-specific. The price-segment data shows the sub-CHF 200 segment held up at +1.0%, but Above CHF 3,000 fell 15.6% — exactly where Swatch's premium brands play."
- **4:30–5:00 — Click Report:** "Full nine-section audit on this last page — executive summary, going concern, three red flags broken out, two amber flags, auditor's overall opinion. Bottom line: PwC says the financials are fair, but the *business model* shows structural strain. Equity ratio of 87% buys them time to fix it. Verdict: not a solvency event, but earnings will not mean-revert without a cost restructuring or a China recovery."

**Strengths of this path:**
- The Dashboard alone covers 80% of the thesis — the student can survive even if they get nervous and click nothing else.
- The 5.1× callout gives a memorable single-number anchor (great for "the professor will quote this back").
- Headlines on every page are presenter-ready ("Two Segments. One Country Did the Damage" is a slide title, not a chart caption).

**Friction points:**
- The Dashboard's red-flags grid lists six items but the Report only breaks out three reds + two ambers — the count mismatch will get questioned.
- The Financials page is genuinely *boring* to present from in this format (it's a three-column table). A student would skip it, which means 1/6 of the site is dead weight in a 5-minute talk.
- No "Conclusion" page means the student has to manufacture closure themselves — most will trail off at the Report page rather than land the punchline.

### Persona B — Financial analyst doing diligence

**Their likely path:** Analyst lands on Dashboard, immediately notes equity ratio 87.3% and PwC unqualified — so this is not a credit risk. They skip Financials (they already have the AR open in another tab) and jump straight to **Ratios** to validate the operating leverage claim, then **Segments** to size the China exposure, then **Benchmarking** to determine whether this is idiosyncratic or industry. They open the **Report** last as a reference document.

**Where they get stuck:**
- The Ratios page shows DuPont and operating leverage but **does not show segment-level margins broken out by quarter or by brand tier**. An analyst wants to know: was the margin collapse uniform across W&J, or concentrated in the premium brands? The page can't answer that.
- The Benchmarking page shows peer revenue/EBIT/margin but uses "FY2024 (or nearest comparable)" — for a real diligence read, the analyst needs to know which peer is on a calendar year vs fiscal-year-Jun and whether comps are FX-adjusted. The footnote is too soft.
- No DCF, no scenario table, no normalized-earnings view. The site tells them what *happened*; it does not help them model what *will* happen.
- The CHF 2.1B off-balance-sheet lease commitment is flagged but never quantified as a present-value adjustment to net debt. An analyst wants the IFRS-16 adjusted leverage view.

**Missing for them:**
- A "Sources" or "Reconciliation" link from every metric back to the audited statement page reference.
- A downloadable CSV / Excel of the underlying data.
- An adjusted-EBITDA bridge (reported → adjusted) — critical for a comparable analysis.
- Forward sensitivity: "If China recovers 50%, operating margin reverts to X%."

### Persona C — Journalist looking for a story

**Headline they'd write after 10 minutes:** *"Swatch's China Hangover: How One Country Wiped Out Three-Quarters of Profit at the Maker of Omega — And Why the Dividend Is Now Coming Out of Reserves."*

**What they'd quote:**
- "Every 1% of revenue lost became 5.1× of operating profit lost." (The whole story compressed into a tweet.)
- "Greater China collapsed −30.4% … accounting for ~69% of the group's total revenue decline."
- "Proposed dividend CHF 4.50 vs EPS CHF 3.74 — drawn from reserves."
- "Swatch holds CHF 7,641M of inventory representing 54.6% of total assets" — flagged by PwC as the sole Key Audit Matter.
- The Big Four (Rolex, Patek, AP, Richemont) gained +300 bps while Swatch lost 200 bps — turn this into a "rich-get-richer in luxury watches" angle.

**What they'd miss:**
- The site has no founder/CEO/family quote, no narrative about the **Hayek family** controlling structure, no labor angle (Swatch is a major Swiss employer — how many jobs at risk?). For a journalist, the *human* layer is absent. The financials are clinical.
- No competitive narrative beyond Richemont — the journalist would want the **Apple Watch** angle (Tissot is in the watch market that the Apple Watch ate) but the site doesn't connect Electronic Systems' decline to wearables.
- No history. Why does Swatch hold so much inventory? The 54.6% number is shocking on its own, but the **"smoothing for cyclicality" / vertical integration / movement-supply-to-competitors** backstory would make the article richer.

## Dimension scores

### 1. Entry point — 8/10
The Dashboard is the strongest page on the site. The headline ("The Operating Leverage Crisis"), the four KPI cards, the 6-year revenue trend with 2024 painted rust-red, the 5.1× callout, the six red flags, and the PwC auditor quote together deliver a complete thesis in one viewport. The CTA navigation footer is well-conceived. Loses points only because it gives away the punchline so completely that the rest of the site has nothing left to *reveal*.

### 2. Narrative arc — 6/10
The intended Setup → Evidence → Cause → Diagnostic → Context → Deep-read sequence is sound, but the Dashboard pre-empts the diagnostic moment. The Ratios page is meant to be the "aha" but the 5.1× is already on the Dashboard. The arc has good bookends and weak middle escalation.

### 3. Inter-page handoffs — 4/10
Only the Dashboard has a deliberate "what's next" CTA grid. Financials, Segments, Ratios, and Benchmarking end with content and let the user fall back on the navbar — there's no "Continue to Segments →" prompt at the bottom of Financials, no "See the peer comparison →" at the bottom of Ratios. The Dashboard also has a single inline link to `/report` from the red-flags header, which is good but isolated. The narrative depends on the user knowing to read the navbar left-to-right.

### 4. Climax / payoff — 5/10
The Dashboard's 5.1× callout *is* the climax — and it happens 60 seconds into the experience. Everything after is exposition and validation. There is no second climactic moment engineered into Ratios (where it should live) or into the Report (where the verdict should land with weight). The Report page begins with prose, not a verdict-graphic.

### 5. Resolution — 5/10
The Report page is a long-form artifact — good for depth, weak as a finale. There is no dedicated "Conclusion" / "Verdict" / "Final assessment" page or section that closes the loop on the Dashboard's framing. A reader exits the site without a clear sentence answering "so what should I think?" The Dashboard asked a dramatic question; the site does not deliver a dramatic answer.

## Top 3 narrative wins

- **The Dashboard headline + 5.1× callout combo is genuinely great.** "The Operating Leverage Crisis" frames the entire audit as a single thesis, and the oversized 5.1× rust-colored number gives the eye a place to land. This is the kind of editorial decision that elevates the work from "report" to "story."
- **Every page has a presenter-ready headline.** "Two Segments. One Country Did the Damage." / "Numbers that explain the collapse" / "Swatch underperformed — but not in isolation." These are not chart captions; they are arguments. A student reading the page titles in order gets a coherent five-sentence thesis.
- **The PwC unqualified-opinion callout on the Dashboard is a brilliant tension-setter.** It tells the reader: "the auditors say this is fine — *but here are six red flags we want you to see anyway*." That sets up the entire audit as a layered second-opinion exercise, which is exactly what an audit-class deliverable should feel like.

## Recommendation

**Keep the route order — it works.** The navbar sequence Dashboard → Financials → Segments → Ratios → Benchmarking → Report is correct. Do not reorder.

**Add one new page — `/verdict`.** Insert it as the final route, after Report. It should be a single-viewport page with: (a) a one-sentence audit verdict in serif type, (b) a traffic-light grid across Solvency / Earnings Quality / Governance / Disclosure / Going Concern, (c) the 5.1× number one last time as a "this is what to remember" anchor, (d) a "what to watch in FY2025" two-bullet forward look. This closes the emotional loop the Dashboard opens.

**Do not combine pages.** Each of the six existing pages earns its place. The temptation to fold Segments into Ratios or Benchmarking into Report should be resisted — the *separation* is what makes each page presentable as a single slide.

**Add bottom-of-page CTAs everywhere.** Mirror the Dashboard's footer pattern: at the bottom of Financials, show "Next: Segments → / Jump to: Report →"; at the bottom of Ratios show "Next: Benchmarking → / Back to: Segments →". This converts the navbar from "table of contents" into "guided tour."

**One structural rebalance:** Move the *derivation* of 5.1× off the Dashboard and onto Ratios. The Dashboard should pose the question ("Why did a 14.6% revenue drop become a 74.5% profit collapse?"); Ratios should answer it. Right now both pages answer it, which means neither page surprises the reader.

The site is at 6.5/10 today. With the verdict page, the inter-page CTAs, and the climax-relocation, it gets to 8.5/10 — and becomes the rare student audit that reads as a story rather than a folder of charts.
