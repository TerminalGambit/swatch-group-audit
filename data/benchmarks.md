## 1. Richemont (CFR.SW) — FY2025 Results (Year Ended 31 March 2025)

**Source:** Richemont FY25 Annual Results press release, 16 May 2025 (richemont.com)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Group Sales | €21.399 billion (+4% YoY, actual and constant FX) |
| Operating Profit | €4.467 billion (-7% actual; -4% constant FX) |
| Operating Margin | 20.9% (down 240 bps from prior year) |
| Net Income (continuing ops) | €3.762 billion (-1%) |
| Total Net Income (incl. discontinued) | €2.750 billion (+17%) |
| Net Cash Position | €8.257 billion (+€807m YoY) |
| Proposed Dividend | CHF 3.00 per A-share (+9%) |
| EPS (diluted) | €4.671 |

### Segment Breakdown

**Jewellery Maisons** (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels):
- Sales: €15.3 billion (+8%)
- Operating profit: €4.9 billion (+4%)
- Operating margin: ~32% (31.9%)

**Specialist Watchmakers** (IWC, Panerai, Jaeger-LeCoultre, Vacheron Constantin, A. Lange & Söhne):
- Sales: €3.28 billion (-13% actual and constant FX)
- Operating profit: €175 million
- Operating margin: **5.3%** (down from 15.2% in FY2024)
- Prior year FY2024 sales: €3.8 billion (operating margin was 15.2%)
- Asia-Pacific ex-Japan: -27%
- U.S.: +15%

**Other (Fashion & Accessories):**
- Sales: €2.8 billion (+7%)
- Operating loss: €102 million (-3.7% margin)

### Valuation (as of late 2024)
- P/E ratio: ~24.9–26.7x (trailing twelve months, per TradingEconomics/GuruFocus)
- EV/EBITDA: ~17.5x (TTM ended Sep 2024, GuruFocus)

### Key Notes for Audit
- Richemont's **watch division margin collapsed from 15.2% → 5.3%** in FY2025 — directly comparable to Swatch's watch-only margin of 10.6%. This shows watch-specific pressure is industry-wide.
- The stark contrast between Jewellery Maisons (+8% growth, 32% margin) vs. Specialist Watchmakers (-13%, 5.3%) underscores that **pure-play watchmakers are disproportionately hit** relative to diversified luxury groups with jewellery exposure.
- Richemont's overall group margin (20.9%) is propped up by Cartier/Van Cleef jewellery.

## 9. Audit Analysis: Key Questions Answered

### Q1: Did Swatch underperform vs. peers in 2024? By how much?

**Yes — severely.** Comparison on revenue growth:

| Company | Revenue Growth 2024 | Op. Margin 2024 |
|---------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Swatch Group (total) | **-14.6%** (reported FX) | **4.5%** |
| Swatch Group (Watches & Jewelry seg.) | ~-14% | 10.6% |
| Richemont Specialist Watchmakers | -13% | 5.3% |
| LVMH Watches & Jewelry | -2% (organic) | ~14.7% |
| Swiss industry exports | -2.8% | n/a |
| Hermès (all categories) | +15% | 40.5% |

Swatch's revenue decline (-14.6%) is 5–12 percentage points worse than the Swiss export index (-2.8%) and LVMH watches (-2%). However, Richemont's watch-only segment (-13%) is nearly identical — suggesting Swatch's deterioration is partially comparable to other pure-play watchmakers.

**The key structural difference:** LVMH and Richemont have major jewellery divisions (Cartier, Van Cleef, Bulgari, Tiffany) that offset watch weakness. Swatch is almost entirely watches (+ some jewellery via Harry Winston). This is a structural, not just cyclical, underperformance.

### Q2: What was the industry-wide China impact vs. Swatch-specific?

**Industry-wide:** China exports fell -25.8% (FH 2024 data). Hong Kong -18.7%. The entire Swiss watch industry was China-exposed.

**Swatch-specific:** Greater China (incl. HK and Macau) was **27% of Swatch's turnover** (CHF 1.83B vs. 2.63B in 2023 = -30.4% decline in that region). This is moderately above the industry's 25.8% China export decline but close.

**Swatch's amplification factors:**
- Heavier mid-market exposure (Longines, Rado, Tissot) — mid-priced exports fell -15.6% vs. +1.0% for high-end
- Brands like Breguet (ultra-luxury, China-heavy clientele) saw units fall from 20,000 to 7,400 — worse than industry average
- Omega declined ~8% (less severe than Breguet but still meaningful)
- The below-CHF-3,000 price segment (where much of Swatch's volume sits) fell -15.6% vs. +1.0% for above CHF 3,000

**Conclusion:** China explains ~60–70% of the underperformance; the remaining gap reflects Swatch's specific mid-market price exposure and brand portfolio mix.

### Q3: How does Swatch's 4.5% operating margin compare to peers?

| Entity | Operating Margin 2024 |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Swatch Group (consolidated)** | **4.5%** |
| Swatch Group (Watches & Jewelry only) | 10.6% |
| Richemont Specialist Watchmakers | 5.3% |
| Richemont (group total, FY2025) | 20.9% |
| LVMH Watches & Jewelry | ~14.7% |
| LVMH (group total) | 23.1% |
| Hermès | 40.5% |

**The 4.5% consolidated margin** is distorted by the deliberate production segment loss (Swatch maintained capacity and jobs at cost). The Watches & Jewelry segment margin of 10.6% is a better like-for-like figure. Even so, it is slightly above Richemont's watch-only 5.3% but far below LVMH's 14.7%.

LVMH's higher watch margin reflects Bulgari and Tiffany's jewellery premium, stronger brand mix, and smaller Chinese revenue concentration.

### Q4: Is Swatch losing market share or is this a cyclical downturn?

**Both structural and cyclical — but structural concerns are growing.**

**Cyclical evidence:**
- Entire industry contracted in 2024; Swiss exports -2.8%, China -25.8%
- Richemont's watch division also fell 13% with margin collapse to 5.3%
- Recovery signs: Swatch's Q4 2024 showed improving trend; management guided for "more positive 2025"

**Structural evidence:**
- Swatch market share: **18.3% in 2024, down from ~20.3% in 2023** (200 bps loss in one year)
- The "Big Four" ultra-luxury independents gained 300 bps to 47% combined share
- High-end segment (>CHF 50,000) represented 84% of 2024 export growth — Swatch barely participates here
- Breguet unit collapse (20K → 7.4K) suggests brand equity erosion beyond China alone
- Omega (~CHF 2.4B estimated, ~35% of group sales) stagnating at CHF 2.2B for "more than a decade" per Morgan Stanley
- Industry polarizing toward either ultra-luxury (Rolex, Patek, Richard Mille) or fashion-watches (Apple Watch competition at low end) — Swatch's mid-luxury sweet spot is under structural squeeze
- Swatch Group's 72% volume share in entry/mid-level is losing economic value as that segment shrinks

**Assessment:** ~60% cyclical (China recovery will help), ~40% structural (brand repositioning needed at Omega and Harry Winston; mid-market faces secular pressure).
