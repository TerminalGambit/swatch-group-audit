## Year-by-Year Narrative

### 2019 — Pre-COVID Baseline

Net sales reached CHF 8,243 million, establishing the high-water mark that the group has not recovered to through 2024. This represented a modest decline of 1.8% at constant exchange rates versus 2018, with a currency headwind of CHF 76 million (0.9% of sales). The operating result of CHF 1,023 million and 12.4% operating margin reflected a structurally healthy business, though it was already under pressure: the prior year (2018) had delivered 13.6% operating margin, and the decline was attributed primarily to Hong Kong's protest-related disruption, which eliminated approximately CHF 200 million of revenue in H2 2019. Net income of CHF 748 million carried a 9.1% net margin, down from 10.2% in 2018.

The dividend of CHF 8.00 per bearer share (unchanged from 2018) was the highest in the company's history to that point. The group employed approximately 36,100 persons. In January 2019, Swatch Group formally closed its previous CHF 1 billion share buyback program (launched in 2016), which had repurchased 15,488,500 shares over three years.

**Strategic context:** The Calvin Klein license (22-year relationship covering watches and jewelry) was still active. Harry Winston, acquired in 2013 for CHF 711 million, remained the group's ultra-luxury anchor. The Hong Kong political situation was the primary operational headache — a precursor to the far more severe disruption to come.

---

### 2020 — COVID-19: First Loss in Company History

The COVID-19 pandemic produced the most severe shock in Swatch Group's 37-year history. Net sales collapsed to CHF 5,595 million — a fall of CHF 2,648 million or 32.1% at current exchange rates (28.7% in constant currency). The group posted an operating result of just CHF 52 million (0.9% margin) and a net loss of CHF -53 million: the first loss since the group's founding in 1983.

The pandemic's impact was asymmetric across the year. The first half saw sales decline 43.4% at constant rates as lockdowns simultaneously shuttered manufacturing, closed retail (384 locations temporarily shut), and eliminated tourist spending. The second half recovered substantially — H2 2020 fell only 14.3% versus H2 2019 — driven by China's earlier reopening and a 70% surge in e-commerce (mid and basic segments reached 20-30% online sales penetration). Net liquidity actually improved to CHF 1,700 million (+CHF 332 million versus 2019), reflecting aggressive cost controls and operating cash flow of CHF 819 million.

The workforce contracted by 10.2% to 32,400 persons through natural attrition, store closures, and the wind-down of Calvin Klein operations. The dividend was cut sharply to CHF 3.50 per bearer share (from CHF 8.00) — a 56% reduction that preserved approximately CHF 200 million of cash.

**Strategic context:** Management signaled the end of the Calvin Klein licensing relationship, which was scheduled to be discontinued during 2021. Excluding the Calvin Klein drag, the operating result would have been CHF 99 million rather than CHF 52 million — a meaningful difference. The COVID crisis accelerated Swatch Group's digital transformation and validated its strategy of owning retail infrastructure rather than relying on multi-brand wholesale.

---

### 2021 — V-Shaped Recovery: Back to Profitability

Net sales rebounded to CHF 7,313 million — up 30.7% at current rates and 29.6% at constant rates — as pent-up consumer demand, accumulated savings, and the China recovery drove luxury watch purchases globally. The operating result recovered almost exactly to pre-pandemic levels at CHF 1,021 million, and the operating margin of 14.0% actually exceeded 2019's 12.4%, reflecting operating leverage as volumes recovered over a fixed cost base that had been reduced in 2020.

Net income of CHF 774 million (10.6% net margin) marked a complete earnings recovery. The dividend was partially restored to CHF 5.50 per bearer share. Employees edged down slightly to 31,444 — a 3% decline from 2020 — as the Calvin Klein headcount was removed from the books.

**Critical caveat:** The 2021 recovery, while dramatic in percentage terms, still left net sales 11% below the 2019 peak (CHF 7,313M vs CHF 8,243M). In constant currency terms, which Swatch Group emphasizes in its reporting, the gap was somewhat smaller — but the nominal sales level was not recaptured until after 2021.

**Strategic context:** The Calvin Klein license formally ended in 2021 after 22 years. The brand had been manufactured by Swatch Group since 1997, but management concluded the relationship could not reach its potential given upheaval at Calvin Klein Inc. and PVH. This simplified the brand portfolio and removed a loss-generating unit. Short-time work allowances still cost CHF 61 million in 2021 — declining sharply to CHF 1 million in 2022 — indicating the 2021 recovery was still supported by government labor schemes.

---

### 2022 — Margin Expansion Despite China Headwinds

Net sales grew modestly to CHF 7,499 million (+2.5% at current rates, +4.6% constant FX), with the group now operating closer to — but still below — its 2019 peak. The standout story was margin expansion: operating result of CHF 1,158 million delivered a 15.4% operating margin, the highest in the data series, surpassing even the 2019 pre-COVID level. Net income of CHF 823 million (11.0% net margin) also marked a new peak. The dividend was raised again to CHF 6.00 per bearer share.

This result is remarkable given that China's COVID confinements — China went through its own lockdown wave in 2022 under the Zero-COVID policy — caused sales shortfalls exceeding CHF 700 million versus expectations. Non-China performance compensated fully. The workforce grew by 617 to 32,061 persons, signaling confidence in demand.

**Strategic context:** March 26, 2022 saw the launch of the MoonSwatch — a collaboration between Swatch (CHF 250 price point, bioceramic material) and Omega, styled after the iconic Speedmaster Moonwatch. The launch triggered queues at Swatch boutiques globally and became one of the most talked-about watch launches in decades. CEO Nick Hayek reported that the MoonSwatch halo effect boosted Omega Speedmaster sales by approximately 50%, demonstrating the strategic value of cross-segment internal collaboration. Over one million MoonSwatch units were sold in the year.

---

### 2023 — Peak Profitability, Emerging Clouds

Net sales reached CHF 7,888 million — the highest since 2019 — with growth of 12.6% at constant exchange rates (5.2% at current rates). A significant currency headwind of CHF 554 million (7.4%) masked the underlying volume strength. The operating result of CHF 1,191 million (15.1% margin) and net income of CHF 890 million (11.3% net margin) set record profit levels in absolute terms for the company. The dividend was raised to CHF 6.50 per bearer share.

Headcount increased by 1,541 to 33,602 persons, including 802 new jobs in Switzerland. Capital investments totaled CHF 803 million, reflecting confidence in continued growth — decisions that would look aggressive in hindsight given 2024's sharp reversal. The group's real estate portfolio (manufacturing, retail, offices) carried a historical cost of CHF 3 billion and net depreciated value of CHF 1.8 billion, representing substantial hard-asset backing.

**Strategic context:** While aggregate numbers were strong, the luxury industry's China dependence was becoming a known risk. Late 2023 showed early signs of Chinese consumer demand softening as the post-COVID reopening enthusiasm faded and property market stress weighed on consumer confidence. The group's 2023 H2 sales trajectory pointed to the turbulence ahead.

---

### 2024 — China-Led Collapse (Already Known)

Net sales fell to CHF 6,735 million — a decline of 12.2% at constant FX and 14.6% at current rates. The operating result collapsed to CHF 304 million (4.5% margin) and net income fell to CHF 219 million (3.3% net margin). The dividend was cut to CHF 4.50 per bearer share from CHF 6.50.

The Watches & Jewelry segment's operating margin contracted from 17.2% to 10.6%. The Production segment (supplying components to third-party watchmakers) posted a strongly negative operating result, reflecting the industry-wide production slowdown as inventories built up. Electronic Systems (EM Microelectronics and Renata) reported CHF 330 million in sales and CHF 12 million operating profit, down from CHF 27 million.

Capital investments were reduced from CHF 803 million to CHF 568 million. The workforce declined 2.1% to 32,477, managed primarily through natural attrition. Management began sustained open-market share repurchases exceeding CHF 600 million in aggregate across 2023-2025, and the Hayek family made direct market purchases (July 2024: CHF 14.3 million across Nayla Hayek, Nick Hayek Jr., and Marc Hayek), signaling conviction that the market undervalued the group.

## Trend Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory

The group's revenue profile forms a distinct W-shape over 2019-2024:

- **2019 peak**: CHF 8,243M — pre-pandemic high-water mark
- **2020 trough**: CHF 5,595M — COVID collapse, -32% from peak
- **Recovery arc**: 2021 (+31%), 2022 (+2.5%), 2023 (+5.2%) — approaching but never reaching 2019 level
- **2024 decline**: CHF 6,735M — back to near-2021 levels, driven by China slowdown

The 2024 revenue level represents a 18.3% discount to the 2019 peak, six years later, denominated in nominal CHF. At constant exchange rates, the gap is somewhat larger given CHF appreciation over the period.

### Margin Dynamics

The operating margin story is more encouraging in the middle years:

- **2019**: 12.4% — healthy but declining from 2018 peak
- **2020**: 0.9% — crisis floor
- **2021-2023**: Structurally improved margins (14.0%, 15.4%, 15.1%) exceeding 2019 levels despite lower revenues, reflecting operating leverage, CK exit cost savings, and favorable sales mix toward higher-margin brands
- **2024**: 4.5% — sharp contraction reflecting volume deleveraging on a fixed-heavy cost structure (Swiss manufacturing, owned retail network)

The 2024 margin collapse from 15.1% to 4.5% in a single year is the audit's central focus. It reflects the group's high operating leverage: a 14.6% revenue decline translated into a 74% decline in operating result. The Production division's negative result amplifies this — as the largest supplier of Swiss Made components, it bears the fixed costs of Swiss manufacturing capacity that cannot be quickly scaled down.

### Profitability and Dividend Policy

Net income followed a similar trajectory. The 2020 loss (CHF -53M) was isolated; 2021 recovery to CHF 774M and subsequent growth to CHF 890M in 2023 demonstrated the business model's resilience. The 2024 result of CHF 219M remains positive but represents a 75% decline from 2023.

Dividend policy has been responsive but not aggressive in cutting:

| Fiscal Year | Net Margin | Dividend (Bearer) | Payout vs Net Result |
|------------|-----------|------------------|----------------------|
| 2019 | 9.1% | CHF 8.00 | ~CHF 210M |
| 2020 | (0.9%) | CHF 3.50 | ~CHF 90M (paid from reserves) |
| 2021 | 10.6% | CHF 5.50 | ~CHF 145M |
| 2022 | 11.0% | CHF 6.00 | ~CHF 158M |
| 2023 | 11.3% | CHF 6.50 | ~CHF 170M |
| 2024 | 3.3% | CHF 4.50 | ~CHF 118M |

The 2020 dividend was paid from accumulated reserves — a signal of financial strength and confidence in recovery. The 2024 cut (CHF 6.50 → CHF 4.50) is disciplined but not crisis-level, consistent with management's expectation of recovery in 2025.

| 2020 | 32,400 | -10.2% |

The 2020 workforce reduction was swift and significant. The 2021-2023 rebuild reached only 33,602 at peak — still below the 2019 level of ~36,100. The 2024 reduction was managed through natural attrition rather than layoffs, consistent with Swiss labor practices and the Hayek family's long-standing commitment to Swiss manufacturing employment.

## Corporate Governance and Strategic Actions (2019-2024)

### Leadership Stability

Nick Hayek Jr. has served as CEO continuously since January 1, 2003, maintaining strategic consistency throughout the entire audit period. His sister Nayla Hayek has chaired the Board since June 30, 2010. This continuity distinguishes Swatch Group from most large luxury conglomerates and reflects the family's long-term orientation over short-term financial engineering.

In May 2024, Marc A. Hayek (Nick Hayek Jr.'s nephew, son of the late Nicolas G. Hayek Sr.) joined the Board of Directors, formalizing third-generation succession planning.

### Hayek Family Ownership

The family controls the group through a dual-class share structure: registered shares (CHF 0.45 nominal) and bearer shares (CHF 2.25 nominal) at a 1:5 voting-power ratio, allowing the family to convert approximately 25% economic ownership into approximately 43-44% voting rights. As of end-2023, the Hayek Pool controlled 43.3% of voting rights (62.5 million registered shares + 738,026 bearer shares). By end-2024, this had increased to 44.1%.

The family's July 2024 open-market purchase of 80,371 bearer shares at CHF 178.30 per share (CHF 14.3 million total, involving Nayla, Nick, and Marc Hayek) came at a multi-year low for the stock and was widely interpreted as a confidence signal.

### Share Buybacks

- **2016-2019**: CHF 1 billion program (15,488,500 shares) — formally closed January 24, 2019.
- **2020-2022**: No formal buyback program during COVID and recovery.
- **2023-2025**: Sustained open-market repurchases exceeding CHF 600 million in aggregate. These reduced the public float and mechanically increased the Hayek family's relative voting weight.

### Key Transactions and Strategic Events

**Calvin Klein exit (2021):** The 22-year licensing agreement for Calvin Klein watches and jewelry was allowed to expire. Swatch Group had held this license since 1997, manufacturing and distributing over 200 cK models in 60+ countries at CHF 150-700 price points. The exit removed a loss-generating unit (estimated CHF 47 million operating drag in 2020) and simplified the brand portfolio toward higher-margin proprietary brands.

**MoonSwatch launch (March 26, 2022):** The Swatch-Omega collaboration, producing a Bioceramic version of the Speedmaster Moonwatch at CHF 250, became a cultural phenomenon. Queues formed globally; initial production could not meet demand; over one million units were sold in the first year. The halo effect reportedly boosted Omega Speedmaster sales by ~50% and demonstrated the latent brand equity in Swatch Group's multi-brand architecture when segments collaborate.

**Harry Winston (ongoing):** Acquired in 2013 for CHF 711 million, Harry Winston remains the group's ultra-high-net-worth jewelry and watch anchor, participating in the luxury boom of 2021-2023 and providing partial resilience against the 2024 downturn through its ultra-affluent client base.

## Key Audit Observations

1. **Operating leverage is the central risk.** A 14.6% revenue decline in 2024 produced a 74% operating result decline. This reflects the group's strategic choice to own Swiss manufacturing infrastructure and retail networks — fixed-cost-intensive but strategically valuable in good times.

2. **The 2019 peak has not been recovered.** Despite record margins in 2022-2023, the nominal revenue peak of CHF 8,243 million (2019) has not been reached six years later. In real terms (accounting for CHF appreciation and inflation), the gap is larger.

3. **China concentration is the unhedged risk.** The 2020 recovery was disproportionately China-driven; the 2024 collapse is disproportionately China-driven. The group's geographic concentration represents the primary systemic risk factor over the audit horizon.

4. **Financial strength provides a buffer.** Net liquidity of CHF 1,700 million as of end-2020 demonstrates the balance sheet's resilience. The group can sustain dividend payments (and even a loss year) without distress. The 2024 operating result of CHF 304 million, while poor, generates positive cash flow.

5. **Family governance provides stability and long-term orientation** but also concentration risk and limited external checks on strategic decisions.
